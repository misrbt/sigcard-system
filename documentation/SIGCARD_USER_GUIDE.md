## Part 1 — Getting Started

### 1.1 What is the DIGICUR (Digital Customer Record System)?

In simple words: **it is a digital filing cabinet for the bank's customer signature cards.** Instead of keeping paper signature cards in folders that can get lost, torn, or wet, the bank now stores them safely on the computer. You can search for any customer in seconds, view their signature, ID, and other documents, and add new customers as they open accounts.

The system keeps a log of every action, so the bank can prove to BSP (the central bank of the Philippines) that customer records are safe and properly handled.

### 1.2 What You Need Before You Start

Before you sign in for the first time, make sure you have:

- **A computer** with internet (a desktop, laptop, or even a tablet works).
- **A web browser** — Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge is best. Mozilla Firefox also works.
- **The website link** for SigCard. Your administrator will give you this.
- **Your email address** (the one your administrator registered for you).
- **Your password** — usually a temporary one for your first sign in.

### 1.3 How to Sign In

1. Open your web browser.
2. Type in the SigCard website link, or click the bookmark.
3. You will see a sign-in page that says **"Sign in"** in the middle.
4. Type your **email address**.
5. Type your **password**.
6. Click the blue **Sign in** button.

If everything is correct, the system will take you to your home screen.

> **Tip:** If you get a red error message saying *"Invalid credentials"*, double-check your typing. Capital letters and small letters matter in passwords.

### 1.4 Your First Time Signing In

The very first time you sign in — or anytime your administrator resets your password — the system will ask you to make a new password.

Here is what to do:

1. Sign in using the temporary password your administrator gave you. (For new accounts, this is usually `abc_123`.)
2. The system will show a "Change Password" screen.
3. Type your **temporary password** in the first box (the one labeled "current password").
4. Type your **new password** in the second box. Make it something only you know.
5. Type the **same new password** again in the third box, to confirm it.
6. Click **Change Password**.

After this, you will be taken to your home screen. From now on, sign in with your new password — not the temporary one.

> **Important:** Write your new password down somewhere safe — for example, a locked drawer at home. **Do not** put it on a sticky note on your monitor or share it with anyone.

### 1.5 What Makes a Good Password

Your password must follow these rules:

- At least **8 characters** long
- Contains **at least one capital letter** (A, B, C…)
- Contains **at least one small letter** (a, b, c…)
- Contains **at least one number** (0–9)
- Contains **at least one special character** like `!`, `@`, `#`, `$`, `%`, `&`

**Good examples:** `Banko@2026!`, `Talisayan#9`, `Rbt$ave99`

**Bad examples:** `password`, `12345678`, your birthday, your name. These are too easy to guess.

> **Rules of thumb:**
> - Never tell anyone your password. Not even a co-worker. Not even someone who calls and says they are from IT.
> - Change your password if you think someone may have seen it.
> - Use a different password from the ones you use for Facebook, Gmail, etc.

### 1.6 Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Some accounts have an extra layer of security called **two-factor authentication**. If yours is turned on, here is what happens:

1. You sign in with your email and password as normal.
2. Instead of going straight to your home screen, the system asks for a **6-digit code**.
3. The code is sent to your registered phone or email. Open it and read the 6 numbers.
4. Type the 6 numbers into the box.
5. Click **Verify**, and you will be taken to your home screen.

> **Why this matters:** Even if someone steals your password, they still cannot get into your account without the 6-digit code from your phone. It is a powerful protection.

### 1.7 Signing Out the Right Way

When you are done using SigCard, **always sign out**. Do not just close the browser tab.

1. Look at the **top right corner** of the screen. You will see your name or a small circle with your initials.
2. Click on it. A small menu will drop down.
3. Click **Logout**.

You will be returned to the sign-in page.

> **Why this matters:** If you walk away without signing out, someone else could sit at your computer and pretend to be you. Always sign out — even just to go to lunch.

### 1.8 What Happens If You Walk Away from Your Desk

For your protection, the system watches whether you are using it.

- If you **do not move the mouse, type, or click for 10 minutes**, the system signs you out by itself.
- When this happens, you will be sent back to the sign-in page.
- Any work you had not yet saved (for example, an upload you had not finished) may be lost.

> **Tip:** If you need to step away for more than a couple of minutes, finish what you are doing and sign out manually.

### 1.9 If You Forget Your Password or Get Locked Out

It happens to everyone. Don't panic.

- If you type the wrong password, the system will tell you to try again.
- After **5 wrong tries**, the system will lock your account for **30 minutes** to keep it safe.
- If you cannot remember your password, **stop guessing** and ask your administrator to reset it.

When the administrator resets your password, they will give you the temporary password (`abc_123`) again. Sign in with it, and the system will ask you to make a new one — same steps as Section 1.4.

## Part 3 — Banking Officer Guide

You are the heart of the system. You handle most of the customer paperwork — uploading signature cards, adding new customers, scanning IDs, and keeping records up to date.

### 3.1 Your Home Screen

When you sign in, you will see a welcome page with a dark blue navigation bar at the top. The bar has three buttons:

- **Home** — your starting page.
- **Upload** — where you add a new customer and their signature card.
- **Customer Profiles** — where you search for and view existing customers.

In the top right corner, you will see your name with a small circle. Click it anytime to view your profile or sign out.

### 3.2 The Big Task: Uploading a Signature Card

This is the main job you will do every day. The upload is a step-by-step "wizard" — the system walks you through it one page at a time. You can always click **Back** to fix something.

**Before you start, get these things ready:**

- The customer's signed signature card (the physical paper).
- The customer's signed Data Privacy Consent form.
- A camera or scanner (a phone camera works, as long as the photo is clear).
- The customer's full name and account number.
- (Optional) Any IDs, NAIS forms, or other supporting documents.

Now, here is how to do it:

#### Step 1 — Click "Upload"

From the navigation bar at the top, click **Upload**. You will be taken to the upload wizard.

#### Step 2 — Pick the Account Type

The first page asks: **what kind of account is this?** You will see three big tiles:

- **Regular** — A normal individual account. One person owns it. *Example: Maria Santos opens a savings account in her name only.*
- **Joint** — Two or more people share one account. *Example: A husband and wife open one savings account together.*
- **Corporate** — A business or organization. *Example: A barangay cooperative or a small company.*

Click the tile that matches your customer.

#### Step 3 — (Joint Only) Pick the Joint Type

If you chose **Joint**, the system will now ask: **ITF or Non-ITF?**

- **ITF (In Trust For)** — One person holds the account in trust *for* another person. The most common example is a parent opening an account for their child: the parent manages it, but the money belongs to the child. Each person uploads their own documents.
- **Non-ITF** — All the people are equal co-owners. Any of them can transact. They share documents.

Pick the right one. If you are not sure, ask the customer or your branch manager.

#### Step 4 — Enter Customer Information

Type the customer's details in the form:

- **First name**
- **Middle name** (optional)
- **Last name**
- **Suffix** like Jr., Sr., III (optional)
- For **Corporate** accounts, you will type the **company name** instead.
- For **Joint** accounts, you can add more co-signers by clicking the **+ Add another holder** button.

When done, click **Next**.

#### Step 5 — Set the Account Holder Details

For each account holder, fill in:

- **Account Number** — the bank account number printed on the signature card.
- **Date Opened** — when the customer first opened this account.
- **Risk Level** — choose **Low Risk**, **Medium Risk**, or **High Risk**. If you are unsure, your branch manager will advise. (Most regular customers are Low Risk.)
- **Account Status:**
  - **Active** — the account is in normal use.
  - **Dormant** — no transactions for a long time.
  - **Reactivated** — was dormant, but recently used again.
  - **Escheat** — abandoned or unclaimed (rare).
  - **Closed** — the account has been closed.

If the customer has more than one account, click **+ Add another account** and fill in the details for the next one.

When done, click **Next**.

#### Step 6 — Upload the Signature Card (Sigcard Upload)

Now the actual signature card images. You need two:

- **Front of the signature card**
- **Back of the signature card**

For each, you can either:
- **Drag and drop** the picture file into the box, or
- **Click the box** to browse your computer and pick the file.

> **Tips for a good photo:**
> - Take the picture in **good light**, but no glare on the paper.
> - Make sure **all four corners of the card are visible**.
> - Keep the card **flat** and the camera **straight above it**.
> - The signature must be **clear and readable** — not blurry.
> - If the photo is bad, you can re-take it and upload again.

A green checkmark will appear once each photo is selected. Click **Next**.

#### Step 7 — Upload the NAIS (Optional)

NAIS is the **National Account Information Sheet**. It is the form where the customer's basic ID and personal info is recorded.

- If the customer signed a NAIS form, upload the **front and back** images here.
- If you do not have a NAIS form, you can **skip this step** by clicking **Next**.

#### Step 8 — Upload the Data Privacy Consent (Required)

Every customer must sign a Data Privacy form — this is a Philippine legal requirement. Upload the **front and back** images of the signed form.

> **Why this matters:** The Data Privacy Act of 2012 requires the customer's written consent before the bank can store their personal information. **Do not skip this step.**

#### Step 9 — Other Documents (Optional)

If the customer brought any other supporting papers — for example, a special power of attorney, a birth certificate for a minor's ITF account, or a business permit — upload them here. You can upload several files.

If there are no other documents, click **Next**.

#### Step 10 — Submit

The last page shows a summary of everything you entered. Read it carefully.

- If you spot a mistake, click **Back** to fix it.
- If everything looks right, click **Submit**.

The system will save the customer and all their documents. You will see a success message.

> **What if it fails?** If you see a red error message, write down what it says and tell your administrator. The most common reason is a slow internet connection or a file that is too big.

### 3.3 Looking Up an Existing Customer

1. From the top bar, click **Customer Profiles**.
2. You will see a list (or grid) of customers you have uploaded.
3. Use the **search box** at the top to type any part of the customer's name or account number.
4. Click on a customer's row to expand it and see their accounts.

### 3.4 Viewing a Customer's Documents

1. From the customer list, click the customer.
2. Click the **View** button.
3. You will see the customer's full profile, with all their accounts and a gallery of all their uploaded images.
4. Click any image to see it large. You can:
   - **Zoom in and out** with the + and − keys (or buttons).
   - **Move the picture around** by clicking and dragging.
   - **Go to the next or previous picture** with the left/right arrow keys.
   - **Close** the viewer with the X button or Esc key.

### 3.5 Editing or Replacing a Document

If you uploaded a blurry or wrong picture, you can fix it:

1. Open the customer's profile.
2. Click **Edit** (or the pencil icon next to the document).
3. Upload a new front or back image to replace the old one.
4. Click **Save**.

The old image is replaced. The system keeps a record that you changed it.

### 3.6 Adding Another Account to an Existing Customer

If a customer who already exists in the system opens a **new account**, do this:

1. Open the customer's profile.
2. Click **Add Account**.
3. The wizard will ask for the new account's number, date opened, risk level, and status.
4. Upload any new documents that go with the new account.
5. Click **Submit**.

The new account is now linked to the same customer.

### 3.7 Searching by Thumbmark (Fingerprint)

If a customer comes to the bank but cannot remember their account number or even their name, you can sometimes find them by their **thumbprint**.

1. From **Customer Profiles**, click **Thumbmark Search**.
2. The customer presses their thumb on the fingerprint reader (if your branch has one) — or you can scan/photograph it.
3. The system will look for any matching signature card in its records.

> **Important:** This only works if the original signature card had a clear thumbmark on it. Not every signature card includes one. If the search does not find a match, do not worry — just use the regular name search instead.

### 3.8 Your Profile

To view or update your own information:

1. Click your name in the **top right corner**.
2. Click **My Profile**.

You will see your name, email, role, branch, when you last signed in, and whether two-factor authentication is on.

You can change your password from here too. See [Part 8](#part-8--common-tasks-for-everyone).

## Part 4 — Cashier Guide

Your role is to **look up customer information quickly** so you can serve customers at the teller window. You can view, but you cannot upload or change anything — that is the Banking Officer's job.

### 4.1 Your Home Screen

When you sign in, you will see a top navigation bar with three buttons:

- **Dashboard** — branch summary numbers.
- **Customers** — the customer list for your branch.
- **Documents** — the documents on file at your branch.

### 4.2 Your Dashboard

The dashboard shows you at a glance how busy your branch is:

- Total number of customers in your branch.
- Total signature cards on file.
- Total documents.
- How many uploads were done today.

These numbers help you and your manager track activity.

### 4.3 Looking Up a Customer

1. Click **Customers** in the top bar.
2. Use the **search box** to type the customer's name or account number.
3. Click on the customer to view their profile.
4. You will see all their accounts and a gallery of all their documents.

### 4.4 Viewing a Customer's Documents

1. Click the customer's name to open their profile.
2. Click any document image to view it full-size.
3. Use the + and − keys to zoom; click and drag to move the image around; arrow keys to flip between images.
4. Press Esc or click the X to close.

### 4.5 What You Cannot Do (and Why)

You will notice you have **no Upload button and no Edit buttons**. This is on purpose:

- Adding new customers and uploading signature cards is the Banking Officer's job. This keeps the work clear and prevents mistakes.
- Your job is to **verify** — to look at the signature card and confirm a customer's identity before completing a transaction.

If you find a mistake (wrong image, missing document, wrong name), tell the Banking Officer. They will correct it.

### 4.6 Your Profile

Click your name in the top right corner, then click **Profile**. You will see your account info and can change your password.

## Part 5 — Branch Manager Guide

You oversee everything that happens at your branch. The system gives you a window into your branch's activity.

### 5.1 Your Home Screen

You will see a top navigation bar with three buttons:

- **Dashboard** — your branch's summary numbers.
- **Customers** — all customers at your branch.
- **Documents** — all documents at your branch.

### 5.2 Your Branch Dashboard

The dashboard shows:

- **Total customers** registered at your branch.
- **Signature cards** on file.
- **Total documents.**
- **Uploads today** — how busy the staff are today.

These numbers update automatically as your team uploads. Use them to track productivity and spot quiet days.

### 5.3 Browsing Customers in Your Branch

1. Click **Customers** in the top bar.
2. The list shows every customer registered at your branch.
3. Use the **search box** to find someone by name or account number.
4. Use the **filters** (if shown) to filter by risk level, account status, or account type.
5. Click any customer to see their full profile and documents.

### 5.4 Viewing Documents

Click **Documents** in the top bar to see a summary of documents uploaded at your branch. Drill down by clicking individual documents to view them.

### 5.5 Your Profile

Click your name in the top right corner, then click **Profile**. From here you can update your information and change your password.

> **Note:** Some manager features (such as approving transactions) may exist on the system but not yet have a screen of their own. Ask your administrator if you need to do specific actions that you cannot find on the menu.

## Part 6 — Compliance Auditor Guide

Your role is to make sure the bank is following the rules — BSP regulations, Data Privacy Act, anti-money-laundering laws. The system lets you see **everything that happens across all 11 branches**.

### 6.1 Your Home Screen

You will see a top navigation bar with three buttons:

- **Dashboard** — bank-wide numbers across all branches.
- **Audit Logs** — the heart of your job: a record of every action taken by every user.
- **Customer Profiles** — view any customer in any branch.

### 6.2 Your Dashboard

The dashboard shows totals for the **entire bank**:

- Total customers across all branches.
- Total signature cards.
- Total documents.
- Uploads today (everyone, every branch).

Use this to spot unusual patterns — for example, one branch suddenly uploading hundreds of new customers in one day.

### 6.3 Reading the Audit Logs

The audit log is your most important tool. **Every important thing that happens in the system is written here**, automatically. You cannot change or delete an audit log entry — that is by design, so it can be trusted as evidence.

To use it:

1. Click **Audit Logs** in the top bar.
2. You will see a list of recent actions, newest first. Each entry shows:
   - **Who** did it (the user's name).
   - **What** they did (signed in, created a customer, uploaded a document, changed settings, etc.).
   - **When** it happened (date and time).
   - **What changed** (for edits — old value vs. new value).

3. Use the **filters** to narrow down what you see:
   - **By date range** — for example, "show me everything from October 1 to October 31."
   - **By user** — pick a specific staff member.
   - **By action type** — for example, only show login activity, or only customer record changes.
   - **By category** — Login Activity, Customer Records, Staff Accounts, Security, System.

4. Click on any log entry to see the full details, including what the data looked like before and after the change.

> **Tip for investigations:** If a customer reports that something is wrong with their record, search the audit log for that customer's name. You will see every change made to their profile, by whom, and when. This is your evidence trail.

### 6.4 Looking at Any Customer in Any Branch

Unlike Banking Officers and Managers, you are not limited to one branch.

1. Click **Customer Profiles**.
2. Search by name, account number, or branch.
3. Click any customer to view their full profile and documents.

Use this to spot-check that uploads are being done correctly — clear photos, complete data privacy forms, correct risk levels.

### 6.5 Your Profile

Click your name in the top right corner, then click **Profile**. From here you can update your information and change your password.

## Part 7 — System Administrator Guide

You manage the system itself — the user accounts, the roles, the settings, the branches. This section explains what each menu does and the most common tasks.

### 7.1 Your Home Screen

You use a different layout from the other roles. On the **left side of the screen** is a sidebar with menu buttons:

- **Dashboard** — bank-wide system summary.
- **Users** — create, edit, and manage staff accounts.
- **Roles & Permissions** — control what each role can do.
- **Audit Logs** — same as the compliance auditor's view.
- **Customer Profiles** — view any customer in any branch.
- **Branches** — manage the list of branches.
- **Data Management** — utility tools for system data.
- **Settings** — change system-wide rules (timeouts, password rules, etc.).

You can collapse the sidebar by clicking the menu icon, to make more room on screen.

### 7.2 Creating a New User (Adding Staff to the System)

When a new staff member joins the bank — or when a teller is promoted to manager — you need to give them a SigCard account.

1. From the sidebar, click **Users**.
2. Click the **+ Add User** (or **Create User**) button at the top right.
3. Fill in the form:
   - **First name**
   - **Last name**
   - **Username** (something short and unique)
   - **Email** — the staff member's work email
   - **Branch** — pick from the dropdown
   - **Role** — pick from: Admin, Manager, User (Banking Officer), Cashier, Compliance Auditor
   - **Two-factor authentication** — turn on if the role is sensitive
4. Click **Save** (or **Create**).

> **Important — the temporary password:**
> The system **automatically** sets the new user's password to `abc_123`. Tell the new staff member this password. The first time they sign in, the system will force them to change it to something only they know. This way, you never see their real password.

### 7.3 Editing, Deactivating, and Reactivating Users

When a staff member changes branches, gets promoted, or leaves the bank:

- **Edit a user:** click the user in the list, click **Edit**, change what is needed, click **Save**.
- **Deactivate** (when someone goes on leave or resigns): click the user, click **Deactivate**. They cannot sign in anymore, but their record stays in the system.
- **Reactivate** (when someone returns): click the user, click **Reactivate**. They can sign in again.
- **Suspend** (a temporary block): same as deactivate, but you plan to reactivate soon.

### 7.4 Resetting a User's Password

When a staff member forgets their password:

1. From **Users**, find the user in the list.
2. Click their row to open it.
3. Click **Reset Password**.
4. The system shows you the new temporary password (`abc_123`).
5. Tell the user this temporary password. They will be forced to change it on next sign-in.

> **Never email or text the password.** Tell the person face-to-face, or have them call you back at a number you trust.

### 7.5 Unlocking a Locked Account

If a user got locked out from too many wrong password tries:

1. From **Users**, find the user.
2. Click their row.
3. Click **Unlock Account**.

They can now try signing in again.

### 7.6 Roles & Permissions

The bank has **5 roles**, and each role can do specific things. To see or change what a role can do:

1. From the sidebar, click **Roles & Permissions**.
2. You will see a grid: roles across the top, permissions down the side. A checkmark means "this role can do this thing."
3. To add or remove a permission, click the box. Click **Save**.

> **Warning:** Be very careful here. Removing a permission from a role can stop staff from doing their job. **Test with one user first** before removing permissions for everyone.

### 7.7 Audit Logs

Same as the Compliance Auditor's view (see Part 6.3). As an administrator, you can also **export** the logs to a file for archiving or sending to BSP.

### 7.8 System Settings

From the sidebar, click **Settings**. Here you control how the system behaves bank-wide. The main settings:

- **Session Timeout (minutes)** — how long a user can be idle before the system signs them out. Default: 10 minutes. Lower is more secure; higher is more convenient.
- **Token Expiration** — how long a sign-in stays valid. Default: 30 minutes.
- **Maximum Login Attempts** — after how many wrong passwords the account locks. Default: 5.
- **Password Expiry** — turn on if you want passwords to expire after some days (forcing users to make new ones). Default: off.
- **Password Expiry Days** — if expiry is on, how many days a password lasts. Default: 90.

Change a setting, click **Save**, and the new rule applies to everyone.

> **Tip:** Talk to the Compliance Officer before changing security settings. BSP has rules about minimums.

### 7.9 Branches

From the sidebar, click **Branches** to see the 11 branches and their codes. You can add a new branch, edit an existing one, or change which branch is the parent of a smaller "BLU" (Branch Lite Unit).

### 7.10 Data Management

This menu has utility tools for system data — for example, importing and exporting bulk data, archiving old records, or cleaning up duplicates. Use this carefully — actions here affect many records at once. Read each tool's confirmation message before clicking.

### 7.11 Your Profile

Click your name in the top right corner, then click **Profile** to update your own information and change your own password.

## Part 8 — Common Tasks for Everyone

These tasks are the same no matter what role you have.

### 8.1 Changing Your Password

1. Click your name in the **top right corner**.
2. Click **Profile** (or **My Profile**).
3. On your profile page, find the **Change Password** section.
4. Type your **current password** in the first box.
5. Type your **new password** in the second box.
6. Type your **new password again** in the third box, to confirm.
7. Click **Change Password**.

The next time you sign in, use your new password.

### 8.2 Updating Your Profile Photo

1. Open your profile (top right corner → **Profile**).
2. Click on your photo (or the empty circle).
3. Pick a picture from your computer.
4. Click **Save**.

> **Tip:** Use a clear photo of yourself, like a passport-style photo. This helps your co-workers recognize you in the system.

### 8.3 Turning On Two-Factor Authentication for Yourself

1. Open your profile.
2. Find the **Two-Factor Authentication** section.
3. Click **Enable**.
4. Follow the instructions on screen — usually scanning a QR code with your phone.
5. Type the 6-digit code from your phone to confirm.

From now on, every time you sign in, you will need your password **and** the code from your phone.

> **Strongly recommended for Admins, Managers, and Compliance Auditors.** Your accounts can do sensitive things, so the extra protection is worth it.

### 8.4 What to Do If the System Seems Frozen

Try these steps in order:

1. **Wait 10 seconds.** Sometimes the internet is slow.
2. **Refresh the page.** Press F5 on your keyboard, or click the circular arrow in your browser.
3. **Sign out and sign back in.** This fixes most small problems.
4. **Try a different browser.** Open Chrome if you were using Edge, or vice versa.
5. **Restart your computer.**
6. **If none of that works**, tell your branch's IT person or system administrator. Be ready to describe **what you were doing when it went wrong**, and **what error message you saw**.

### 8.5 What to Do If a Button or Link Does Not Work

- It may be a feature your role does not have permission to use. Check whether the button is greyed out — that means it is intentionally disabled for your role.
- It may be a temporary glitch. Refresh the page (press F5).
- If it keeps happening, take a screenshot and send it to your administrator.

## Part 10 — Who to Ask for Help

When something goes wrong:

### Step 1 — Try the Easy Fixes First

- **Refresh the page** (F5 key).
- **Sign out and sign back in.**
- **Check your internet connection.**

These three steps fix maybe 7 out of 10 problems.

### Step 2 — Ask Your Co-Workers

- A more experienced co-worker has probably seen the same problem. Ask them.
- Your **branch manager** can help with most day-to-day questions.

### Step 3 — Ask the System Administrator

For these problems, contact your system administrator directly:
- I cannot sign in / I am locked out.
- I forgot my password.
- I see "Permission denied" when I try to do my job.
- The system shows a strange error and the easy fixes did not help.
- I think someone has used my account without my permission.

### Things You Should NEVER Do

- **Never tell anyone your password** — not even someone who calls and says they are from IT. The real IT team will never ask you for your password.
- **Never let someone else sign in with your account.** If they do something wrong, the audit log will show your name.
- **Never write your password on a sticky note** stuck to your monitor or under your keyboard.
- **Never click "Remember Me" on a public computer.**
- **Never share customer information** with people outside the bank, even friends or family.
